Post Q&A
🕹️ Grandpa's Era — Atari & Pong

Today I played video games with Alan, Micah, and Jennifer. We live hundreds of miles apart, but we play online. My grandpa thinks that's amazing. He said when he was my age, he played at a friend's house. They used to plug the game console into a TV and play on a single screen. The first popular game they played at home was Pong, made by Atari. Players "hit" a ball across a line on the screen. Later, gaming hardwares evolved. Grandpa got a small computer and bought games on computer disks. The first games were simple, and all the graphics were black and white. It sounds pretty boring to me, but Grandpa says it was exciting because it was something new.

📺 Dad's Era — Nintendo 64 & PlayStation

At first, software was limited by how many pixels the computer could draw on the screen per second. When my dad started playing video games, computer clock speeds were much faster. Faster clock speeds meant better graphics. Video game consoles didn't connect to the internet yet, though. My dad still had to buy games to play on his Nintendo 64, one of the first consoles with 3D graphics. One of Dad's friends had a PlayStation, and they would argue about whether the Nintendo 64 or the PlayStation had better graphics! Dad loved to play Mario Kart 64 in split screen.

🌐 Today's Era — Online & Independent Games

Big companies like Atari and Nintendo had to spend millions of dollars to make and sell their early games. Today, thanks to the internet, independent game creators can also make and distribute games without the high costs. People can play on the devices they have—phones, tablets, computers, whatever! Alan, Jennifer, Micah and I live in different time zones. But we can get together online and play our favorite games in real time.
